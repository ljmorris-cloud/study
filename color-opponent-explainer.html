Why this matters: the LGN doesn't pass wavelength to your brain directly — it recodes it into two opponent signals, red-vs-green and blue-vs-yellow, plus a third for light-vs-dark. That's why you can imagine reddish-yellow (orange) but never reddish-green: the two ends of one channel can't fire at once. It's also why staring at red and looking away leaves a green afterimage — the channel overshoots the other way once the stimulus is gone.
