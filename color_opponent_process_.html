Each retinal ganglion cell has a center and a ring-shaped surround wired to opposite signs. Light hitting the center excites it; the same light hitting the surround inhibits it — the cell reports the difference. That subtraction, repeated across the retina, is the opponent channel.

05 — what you see

What's plotted: these curves are modeled after the classic hue-cancellation experiments of Hurvich & Jameson — not raw cone output, but the perceptual balance each opponent mechanism reports at every wavelength. The red–green mechanism reads red at both spectral extremes and green in the middle, crossing zero near 477 nm and 580 nm. The blue–yellow mechanism reads blue at short wavelengths and yellow at long ones, crossing near 505 nm. Because each mechanism can only report one pole at a time, you can imagine reddish-yellow (orange) but never reddish-green — the two ends of one channel can't fire at once.
