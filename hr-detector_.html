ELEMENTARY MOTION DETECTOR · REICHARDT CORRELATOR

Why a fly's eye can miss motion — and ignore a flickering light

Eight photoreceptors feed a row of Hassenstein–Reichardt detectors, the wiring thought to underlie motion vision in insects. Each detector delays one receptor's signal and multiplies it against its neighbour's live signal a beat later. Watch the signal slide down the delay line, then try breaking the circuit two ways, or mistune the delay itself.

NORMAL MOTION Press play — the object crosses the array at one receptor per tick.
Model: two mirror‑symmetric correlator subunits per receptor pair — one delays the left input and multiplies by the right input's live value (L→R), the other delays the right input and multiplies by the left input's live value (R→L) — summed across the array and then subtracted, as in the standard Hassenstein–Reichardt / opponent Reichardt‑detector model. Circuit tuning sets τ, the number of ticks a receptor's signal is held in the delay line before release — biologically, this is the detector's preferred temporal frequency.
