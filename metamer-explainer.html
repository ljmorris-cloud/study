Metamers: when different light looks identical

A metamer is two physically different mixes of light that your eyes cannot tell apart — because they push your three color sensors to the exact same totals.

The idea in one breath: imagine you only had three taste buds — sweet, sour, bitter. Two totally different smoothies, made from completely different fruit, could taste identical to you if they hit those three buds equally hard. Your eyes work the same way. You don't see wavelengths directly — you see three totals, added up by three types of cone. If two different light mixes produce the same three totals, your brain reports the same color, even though the light itself is nothing alike.

The soft shaded curves are your cones' sensitivity at each wavelength. The two lines are the actual light hitting your eye: Spectrum A never changes. Spectrum B is built from three ingredients — blue, green and red — that you mix yourself, down in the controls.

2 — What each cone adds up, and 3 — what you end up seeing

Each cone doesn't see the whole spectrum — it sums up everything landing on it, weighted by its own sensitivity, into one single number. If all three of those numbers line up between A and B, the two lights read as the same color, even though they're physically different. That's the whole trick.

Try it: press "Snap to exact metamer" to find a mix of blue, green and red that matches Spectrum A under daylight. Then switch to the tungsten bulb without touching the sliders — watch the match break. The two spectra didn't change. The light did.

Why this matters outside a physics demo: two paint swatches, or two shirts, can be dyed with completely different pigments yet look identically colored under one type of light — store fluorescents, say — and then look mismatched the moment you step outside into daylight. Nothing about the paint changed. Only the light did. Your eyes were never comparing the objects directly — only the three totals your cones could extract, under one particular light. That mismatch, and the match that preceded it, are both called metamerism.
