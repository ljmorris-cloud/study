ELEMENTARY MOTION DETECTOR · REICHARDT CORRELATOR

Why a fly's eye can miss motion — and ignore a flickering light

Eight photoreceptors feed a row of Hassenstein–Reichardt detectors, the wiring thought to underlie motion vision in insects. Each receptor sends its signal two ways: through a delay (D) to the multiplier on its right, and straight, undelayed, to the multiplier on its left. Each multiplier (X) fires only when the delayed signal and its neighbour's live signal arrive together, and M adds them up. Watch the signal wait in D, then try breaking the circuit two ways, or mistune the delay itself.

Model (matches the reference diagram): receptor i feeds its own delay node Dᵢ, and Dᵢ connects to multiplier Xᵢ on its right. Receptor i+1 connects to that same Xᵢ directly, with no delay. X multiplies its two inputs, and M sums every X. This single arm prefers left‑to‑right motion; a full Hassenstein–Reichardt detector pairs it with a mirror‑image arm and subtracts the two (the opponent stage), which also cancels flicker at every tuning. Circuit tuning sets τ, the number of ticks a receptor's signal is held in D before release — biologically, the detector's preferred temporal frequency.
